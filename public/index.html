The problem

Product specifications tell you what a device does. They don't always tell you how long it will be maintained.

Security-support commitments are buried across manufacturer support pages, policy documents and product notices. They are written at different levels of precision, updated without notice, and rarely appear beside the product a customer is considering.

Retailers end up comparing specifications while the part that shapes a device's useful life stays out of view.

A specification sheet lists a battery life. It rarely lists an end-of-support date.

Why it matters

The support period outlives the unboxing

A connected product is never finished at the point of sale. Its usefulness depends on the software behind it, and on how long the manufacturer intends to keep that software maintained.

Software is part of the product

Connected devices depend on firmware, companion apps and cloud services. Their working life is shaped by the updates that keep those parts current.

Vulnerabilities can appear later

A security issue may surface years after a device ships, when the original purchase decision is long settled and the packaging is gone.

Retailers need a comparable answer

Without a published end date, support is difficult to compare across manufacturers, models and price points, and easy to leave out of a product page.

SS Tool records what manufacturers publish. It does not prevent cyberattacks, certify devices as secure, or guarantee that a promised update will be delivered.

FAQ

Questions about the record

Answers reflect what SS Tool currently does, including where it stops.

What is SS Tool?

SS Tool is a security-support intelligence platform for European electronics retailers. It imports product catalogues, finds manufacturer support pages, extracts published security-update commitments, and makes reviewed information available through a public support-card API.

Where does security-support information come from?

From manufacturer-published pages that SS Tool retrieves and stores, such as support, policy and security pages on the manufacturer's website. Each record keeps the source URL and the exact quotation it came from. Third-party commentary is not used as a source.

Does SS Tool verify that manufacturers actually deliver every promised update?

No. SS Tool verifies that a statement appears in a manufacturer source and that the quotation and date are recorded accurately. It does not monitor whether updates are shipped, and it cannot guarantee that a manufacturer will honour a commitment.

What happens when manufacturer sources disagree?

Conflicting commitments are detected and flagged for human review. A support card caught in a conflict is shown as conflicting information; SS Tool does not choose one date over the other or present a single value as settled.

Does SS Tool certify products as secure?

No. SS Tool does not certify devices, issue security ratings, or verify compliance with any standard. It records and reviews manufacturer-published support commitments, with their sources and limitations attached.

Who is SS Tool designed for?

European electronics retailers, e-commerce teams and agencies that manage product catalogues, along with the customers who need a clearer view of how long a device will be supported.
